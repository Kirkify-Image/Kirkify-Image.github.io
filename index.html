Complete Guide to Pixel Art in Tomodachi Life

Living The Grid is a free, browser-based pixel art helper built for Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream. Upload any image and instantly get a numbered, color-coded paint-by-numbers reference calibrated to the game's 84-color palette and 256 × 256 canvas. No installation, no account, and no image data ever leaves your device.

What Is Living The Grid?

Living The Grid converts any uploaded image into a pixel grid that maps directly onto the Palette House drawing canvas. Instead of guessing color positions, Living The Grid tells you exactly which of the 84 in-game swatches goes in each cell — identified by its row-and-column number.

Created by Reddit user ClementGzl shortly after the game launched in April 2026, it quickly became the most recommended resource in communities. It solves the hardest part of Palette House work without requiring any artistic skill. It is entirely free — no premium tiers, no ads, no subscription.

Adjusting Settings & Modes

The side panel offers four controls: Max Colors caps the distinct swatches, while Brightness and Saturation help adjust the image. The Dither toggle applies a Bayer 4×4 pattern blending adjacent colors for smooth gradients.

Choose between Pixel-perfect mode (4, 8, 16, and 32 pixel brushes) for perfectly aligned cells, or Smooth mode (2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 16 pixels) suited for face paint and Mii accessories with soft transitions.

Choosing the Right Source Image

Existing pixel art (classic game sprites, retro mascots) converts with near-perfect fidelity. Cartoon characters with bold outlines and flat fills also produce excellent results.

Photographs of real people are the hardest, as human skin tones require hundreds of subtle shades the 84-color palette cannot represent. Use stylized illustrations instead, crop tightly, and simplify the background.

The Color System & Palette House Setup

We use the Redmean perceptual color-distance formula to find the closest match for each pixel out of the 77 base colors and 7 saturated hues. Every swatch is labeled (e.g., R3·C9) matching its position in pro-artist mode.

Setup Tips: Enable pro-artist mode in the Palette House toolbar. Select the pixel stroke with a square brush head. Work color by color, filling every cell of one color across the canvas before moving to the next. This minimizes color-change time!

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Living The Grid save my images?

No. Everything is processed locally in your browser. No image data ever leaves your device or is stored on any server.

Is Living The Grid affiliated with Nintendo?

No. Living The Grid is an independent fan project not affiliated with or endorsed by Nintendo. Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream is developed and published by Nintendo.

Why does the output look different from my original image?

The game's Palette House is limited to a strict 84-color palette. Living The Grid calculates the closest mathematical match for your original colors, which might result in slight shifting, especially with complex gradients or photos.

Which brush stroke should I use?

Select the pixel stroke with a square brush head in the game. This ensures hard-edged, perfectly square cells that match the generated grid exactly without bleeding across boundaries.

Can I share guides with other players?

Yes! You can save the preview as a PNG and share it with the community. The more players share their files, the richer the shared creative library becomes.

Does it work for face paint and Mii accessories?

Absolutely. We recommend using the "Smooth mode" with smaller brush sizes (like 2px or 4px) for detailed accessories and face paints that require softer transitions.
